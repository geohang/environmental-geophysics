1. Reference: Geometries & Equations

Sphere

Salt Domes, Ore Pods, Cavities

Max Anomaly ($\Delta g_{max}$): $$ \frac{4}{3} \pi G \delta \rho \frac{r^3}{z^2} $$

Depth Rule:
$z \approx 1.305 \cdot x_{1/2}$

Horizontal Cylinder

Tunnels, Pipelines, Antiforms

Max Anomaly ($\Delta g_{max}$): $$ 2 \pi G \delta \rho \frac{r^2}{z} $$

Depth Rule:
$z \approx 1.0 \cdot x_{1/2}$

Vertical Cylinder

Volcanic Plugs, Kimberlite Pipes

Depth Rule (depth to top):
$z_{top} \approx 0.577 \cdot x_{1/2}$

Model assumptionsWhat this simulation simplifies
  • Each mission draws a random depth (20 to 49 m) and a random peak (0.10 to 0.50 mGal) independently, so only the shape of the curve carries depth information.
  • The sphere and the horizontal cylinder are ideal point and line sources, $g \propto 1/(1+(x/z)^2)^{3/2}$ and $g \propto 1/(1+(x/z)^2)$, on a flat, noise-free profile from −200 to 200 m.
  • The vertical cylinder is idealized as a thin, semi-infinite vertical cylinder with its top at depth $z_{top}$, so $x_{1/2} = \sqrt{3}\,z_{top}$ and $z_{top} \approx 0.577\,x_{1/2}$. A finite vertical cylinder response depends on radius and the depths to both its top and base; use a complete forward model when amplitude matters.

Part 1: Theoretical Foundations

Understanding the shape of the Earth and Equipotential Surfaces

The Geoid

The Geoid is the equipotential surface of Earth's gravity potential (gravitational plus centrifugal) that best fits mean sea level (MSL). It is where a still ocean would settle, continued beneath the continents.

Warping:
  • Mass Excess (Mountains): Pulls Geoid UP.
  • Mass Deficiency (Valleys): Pulls Geoid DOWN.

Visualizing the Warp

Part 2: Data Reduction

Correcting raw readings to isolate geological anomalies

Why Reduce Data?

A raw gravimeter reading ($g_{obs}$) includes effects from tides, elevation, and latitude. Data Reduction removes these to find the Bouguer Anomaly.

Temporal Corrections

  • Drift: Correction for instrument spring stretch over time.
  • Tides: Correction for Sun/Moon pull (up to 0.3 mGal).

The "Big Three" Spatial Corrections

1. Latitude Correction ($g_\phi$)

Subtracts theoretical gravity using the GRS67 Formula (fine for teaching; the current reference system is GRS80, with slightly different coefficients):

$$ g_\phi = 9.780318 \left( 1 + 0.0052789 \sin^2 \phi + 0.0000235 \sin^4 \phi \right) \text{ m/s}^2 $$

2. Free-Air Correction (FAC)

Corrects for elevation above sea level.

$\delta g_F = 0.3086 \times h$ (mGal, with $h$ in m)

3. Bouguer Correction (BC)

Subtracts the attraction of the rock slab between the station and sea level.

Formula: $\delta g_B = 0.0419 \times \rho \times h$ (mGal, with $\rho$ in g/cm³ and $h$ in m)

Correction: 11.19 mGal

Terrain Correction (TC)

Hills pull up; Valleys fail to pull down. Both reduce the reading. Therefore, TC is always positive (+).

Isostasy

Large mountain ranges float on the mantle. Airy Hypothesis: Mountains have deep roots. Pratt Hypothesis: Mountains are less dense.

Units Glossary

  • Gal: 1 cm/s².
  • milliGal (mGal): $10^{-3}$ Gal. (Standard).
  • Gravity Unit (g.u.): 0.1 mGal.
  • $\mu$Gal: Micro-gravity surveys.
Model assumptionsWhat this simulation simplifies
  • Theoretical gravity uses the closed GRS67 formula on the reference ellipsoid at sea level; it ignores elevation and local geology.
  • The latitude diagram is schematic: arrow lengths are not to scale, and the gravity arrow points at the Earth's centre instead of along the ellipsoid normal.
  • The Bouguer Anomaly Calculator uses a constant free-air gradient (0.3086 mGal/m) and treats the rock below the station as an infinite flat slab of one density (0.0419 ρ h); it applies no terrain, tide, or drift correction.
  • The Depth Estimator applies fixed half-width rules for ideal sources: z ≈ 1.305 x½ for a sphere and z ≈ x½ for a horizontal cylinder.

1. Electrolytic

Ions move through pore water (dominant in most rocks).

Apply Electric Field

Notice how the large, slow positive (+) and negative (-) ions migrate through the fluid-filled pores.

2. Electronic

Electrons move through conductive minerals (metals, graphite).

Apply Electric Field

Tiny electrons (e⁻) zip rapidly through a continuous solid vein of conductive mineral.

3. Dielectric

Bound charges polarize; dominant at high frequency (GPR, dielectric sensors). IP/SIP instead measures interfacial polarization at grain surfaces.

Apply Electric Field

Charges do not flow continuously. Instead, molecules polarize (stretch and align) in response to the field.

Model assumptionsWhat this simulation simplifies
  • The applied field is uniform, points from left to right, and is simply switched on or off; its strength and frequency cannot be changed.
  • Speeds are illustrative and not to scale: positive and negative ions share one speed range, and electrons are drawn only 3 to 10 times faster than the ions.
  • Ions drift along a straight band that the grains drawn around it do not steer, so pore shape (tortuosity) and conduction along grain surfaces are not shown.
  • In the dielectric panel, each molecule is an electron cloud offset from a positive centre; the offset grows smoothly to a fixed maximum within about half a second and does not depend on frequency. Interfacial polarization at grain surfaces (the IP mechanism) is not animated.

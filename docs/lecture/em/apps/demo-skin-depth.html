Skin Depth Calculator & Visualizer

How deep does an EM field reach? Skin depth sets the answer, and it depends on frequency and ground conductivity.

Try this

Predict
If you lower the frequency 100 times, does the field reach 100 times deeper, 10 times deeper, or 2 times deeper?
Do
  1. Start at the default f = 1 kHz and ρ = 100 Ω·m, and note the skin depth.
  2. Drag f down to 10 Hz and note the skin depth again.
  3. Return f to 1 kHz and raise ρ to 10000 Ω·m.
Explain
Why do a 100-fold drop in f and a 100-fold rise in ρ change δ by the same factor?
–
skin depth δ (field falls to 1/e ≈ 37%)
$\delta = \sqrt{\dfrac{2}{\omega\mu\sigma}} \approx 503\sqrt{\dfrac{\rho}{f}}$ (metres, with $\rho$ in Ω·m and $f$ in Hz). Lower frequency and more resistive ground both push the field deeper.
Small-coil instruments such as the EM31 and EM38, the most common EM tools in environmental work, operate at a low induction number: their skin depth is much larger than the coil spacing, so their depth of investigation is set by the coil spacing and orientation, not by the skin depth.
Model assumptionsWhat this simulation simplifies
  • A plane wave enters a uniform half-space with a single resistivity; there are no layers.
  • Displacement currents are ignored. This holds while σ ≫ ωε, and it fails at the top of the frequency range over resistive ground (for example 1 MHz over 10000 Ω·m), where the field propagates as a radar wave instead of diffusing.
  • The ground is non-magnetic (μ = μ₀).
  • Only the amplitude decay is drawn; the phase, which lags by one radian per skin depth, is not shown.
